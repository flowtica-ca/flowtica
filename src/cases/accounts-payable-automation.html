Accounts Payable Automation (Back-Office Clerk Sprint)

Example · Back Office

Context

Invoices arrive through AP inboxes and vendor portals, then details are retyped into accounting systems. Approvals get chased in email or Teams, and missing PO or vendor details trigger constant back-and-forth. Status visibility is usually weak, so teams spend time answering where each invoice sits instead of clearing work.

This is clerical layer work: repetitive, rules-driven, and measurable. It is ideal for a fixed-scope sprint that automates the happy path end-to-end and leaves ambiguous cases with people.

What this sprint automates

The sprint focuses on complete AP flow coverage for the common path, with exceptions routed to humans.

  • Intake from an AP inbox and optionally a portal export
  • Attachment handling for invoice PDFs plus vendor identification
  • Data extraction into fields: vendor, invoice number, date, amount, tax, PO or cost center, and due date
  • Validation rules for required fields, duplicate detection, mismatch thresholds, and PO-required logic
  • Approval routing using simple policies (amount threshold, cost center, vendor category)
  • Exception queue for missing or low-confidence data, with a human resolution loop
  • Status updates: acknowledged, needs info, approved, posted, and scheduled for payment
  • Sync or export into accounting plus a searchable audit trail

How it behaves in practice

  1. A vendor emails an invoice PDF for $12,480 tied to cost center 410 with PO-99831.
  2. The workflow captures the attachment, identifies the vendor, and extracts invoice fields.
  3. Validation confirms required fields, checks for duplicates, and verifies amount tolerance against the PO.
  4. Because the amount is above threshold, approval is routed to the cost center owner and AP manager.
  5. If PO data is missing, the invoice moves to an exception queue with a clear reason and owner.
  6. Once approved, the workflow posts to accounting (or exports CSV), updates status, and logs every step.

What success looks like

  • Lower manual touches per invoice
  • Shorter cycle time to approved and posted
  • Reduced exception rate over time
  • Fewer duplicate payments and data-entry errors
  • Higher on-time approval completion

Typical delivery plan (2-week sprint)

  1. Day 1–2: map current AP flow, define required fields and rules, and lock success metrics.
  2. Day 3–6: build intake, extraction, validation, and an exception queue with ownership.
  3. Day 7–9: implement approval routing, notifications, and status tracking states.
  4. Day 10: add accounting integration or CSV export, plus audit logging and runbook draft.
  5. Day 11–14: harden retries and alerts, test with real data, launch, and hand off operations.
